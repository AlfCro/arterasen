Every farm unit had a fire house and a milk house. They stand gable to gable, a couple of metres apart. The space between them is called the sval. The fire house roof reaches over it, so people could walk between the houses without getting wet when it rained.

The two houses follow different rules. The fire burned in the fire house, which has openings and a gap near the ground to give the fire a draught. The milk house had to stay cool instead. It is about half the size and lower. It stands on a tight stone base that keeps small animals out, has no smoke hole and never a wooden floor. Its only light comes from a small opening high up on the gable. On some of the milk houses here, that opening is sawn into the shape of a W or a butterfly.

Milk, cream, butter, cheese and messmör, a sweet whey butter, were kept in the milk house. That is why the milk house has a lock and the fire house does not.
