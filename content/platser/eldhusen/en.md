The eldhus, or fire house, was home and kitchen on the summer farm. A fire house measures about four by four metres. Fixed benches run along the walls, where people sat and slept. The hearth lay in the middle of the earth floor, and the smoke escaped through an opening in the roof.

A large copper kettle hung over the fire. In it messmör was boiled for many hours, and the milk was also made into cheese and butter. Several people often shared one fire house.

At the land reform of 1827 there were 17 farm units with 36 owners, and at most there were perhaps 22. Twelve fire houses still stand, and they have several different owners. When the timber was examined in 2024, a log in one of the fire houses had its outermost tree ring from 1646.

Inside, the walls carry many of the farm's carvings. Read more about them on the sign about the carvings. The fire houses are still in use, for get-togethers, games and folk music during the association's culture week.
