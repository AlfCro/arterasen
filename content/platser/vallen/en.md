A fäbod is a summer farm in the forest. Once the snow had melted, the village animals were driven up here so that the meadows back home could grow hay for the winter. During the summer the dairymaids, called fäbodkullor, lived here. They herded the animals, milked them and turned the milk into butter, cheese and messmör, a sweet whey butter.

Ärteråsen was the summer farm of the village of Näset, and its history goes back to the 1400s. Its heyday was in the mid-1800s, when a couple of hundred animals grazed here. The farm was in use until 1954.

Today mountain cattle graze here in summer, and the association keeps the knowledge of summer farm life alive with a culture week every year.

In December 2024, summer farming in Sweden and Norway was added to UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
