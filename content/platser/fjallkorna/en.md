The Swedish mountain cow, fjällko, is an old native breed from northern Sweden and its summer farms. The breed has been kept in the northern Nordic countries for at least a thousand years. It is usually white with black or red patches and has no horns. It does well on forest grazing.

In the past, each farm unit here kept on average about five cows and thirteen goats. Lars Bägerfeldt has worked this out from estate inventories from 1822–1850.

By grazing, the cows keep the meadow open. Without them the land would soon grow over with scrub and forest, and the farm would look completely different. Sheep also graze the meadow in summer.

The cows roam freely on the meadow. Keep your distance and always close gates behind you. Dogs are not welcome in weeks 25–32, while the cows are here.
