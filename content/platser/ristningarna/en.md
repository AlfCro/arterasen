People who stayed on the summer farm liked to leave their mark. With a knife they cut their name or initials into the wall, a year, and often a bomärke, the farm's own sign. Some carvings show hunting scenes with armed men and horses.

The oldest carving that has been found and survives is from 1662. Carvings from the 1660s can be seen in the farm's oldest milk house, where the milk was kept cool. Most of the other carvings are inside the fire houses.

The carvings cannot be replaced. Please look, but never carve into the timber yourself.
