People who stayed on the summer farm liked to leave their mark. With a knife they cut their initials into the wall, a year, and often a bomärke, the farm's own sign. Most carvings are inside the fire houses and on the outer gables of the fire houses and milk houses.

Lars Bägerfeldt, who has studied the summer farm, has counted 10,746 carvings. The most common are the initials of the dairymaids, called fäbodkullor, the women who looked after the animals and the milk in summer. About sixty are pictures: riders, hunting scenes, men with swords or guns, hearts and stars.

The oldest year is above the door on the outside of a milk house: ILS 1662. These are the initials of Jon Larsson of Tillhed, one of the owners of the summer farm at the time.

One of the dairymaids was Brita Eriksdotter from Näset, born in 1833. Her initials BED appear almost a hundred times in the timber. In 1856 she carved SY TYLL BED IAG LÄNGTAR, roughly "Look, Tillhed Brita Eriksdotter. I am longing." Three years later she married, and her carvings stop.

The carvings cannot be replaced. Please look, but never carve into the timber yourself.
